If you are a developer building an enterprise agent, Ampersand enables your agent to reliably work across your customers’ systems of record. More specifically, you can:

- Read historical and current data from your customer’s system.
- Write data to your customer’s system.
- Subscribe to events (creates, deletes, and field changes) in your customer’s system.

Ampersand’s declarative interface and UI components let your customers configure integrations with their CRM, ERP, or other systems of record directly within your product.

[Quickstart →](/starter-project) · [Explore supported systems →](/provider-guides/overview)

## Built for each of your customers’ configurations

**Ampersand handles multi-tenancy at scale.** Say you have 400 customers on Salesforce. Every one of them has customized it to reflect how they run their business, with different custom objects, fields, permissions, and rules for how your product interacts with their data. Two very similar companies can store the same data in completely different places.

Ampersand separates the integration you build from each customer's configuration. You define the schema and actions your application needs once, and each installation keeps track of the per-customer mappings. For example, you define `renewalDate` once, and each customer uses the embeddable UI library to map it to where they're actually storing the renewal date in their Salesforce.

| Customer | Where `renewalDate` lives |
|---|---|
| Customer A | `Account.Renewal_Date__c` |
| Customer B | `Account.Contract_End__c` |
| Customer C | `Subscription__c.End_Date__c` |
| …397 more | Each set by the customer during setup |

Your application reads and writes `renewalDate`. Ampersand translates it to each customer’s field.

## How Ampersand works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Define an integration">
    Use an `amp.yaml` file to declare each integration: the system to connect to, the objects and fields to read or write, and the configuration options to expose to your customers. See the [manifest reference](/manifest-reference).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let customers connect and configure">
    Embed authentication and configuration in your product with [prebuilt UI components](/embeddable-ui-components), [headless UI library](/headless), or [API](/api/overview). Customers connect their own instance and choose field mappings and preferences, and Ampersand saves those choices in their installation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ampersand runs it">

    A managed runtime tracks every customer's configuration and makes the right API calls for each one. It handles authentication, scheduling, retries, rate limits, and non-standard provider error messages. You can either make direct API calls or have async results delivered to your [destinations](/destinations/overview).

  </Step>
  <Step title="Monitor, adapt, and recover">

    Inspect installations and operation logs, get [notifications](/notifications/overview) about connection issues, sync failures, and configuration changes, and [watch for schema changes](/manage-customer-schemas#watch-schema-changes) when fields are added, removed, or modified. Use these signals to operationalize your integrations by alerting your team or your customers when actions need to be taken.

  </Step>
</Steps>
